This is an original illustrative draft about an imagined present-day devotee, not a scriptural account, generated performance, or scored production. Adapt its decisions; do not reuse this narrator/story as the default for every request.

## A revision that changes the song

Weak placeholder:

> मन में अँधेरा, तुम हो उजाला।
>
> शक्ति दे दो, कर दो रखवाला।

The devotion is declared but the listener cannot picture the problem or a changed choice.

More specific verse draft:

> आख़िरी बस गई, मैं हिसाब लिए बैठा,
>
> घर से फ़ोन आया—फिर भी चुप ही रहता।
>
> मंदिर की सीढ़ी पर थककर सिर झुकाया,
>
> पास बैठे बूढ़े को पानी भी न पिलाया।

The narrator's private worry now has a place, a gesture, and a moral blind spot. Let the next verse earn the change through an action:

> जेब में दो सिक्के, हाथ में खाली प्याला,
>
> नल तक गया, लौटकर पानी दे डाला।
>
> बोझ वही था, पर उठने लगा था मैं—
>
> माँ को फ़ोन करके सच कहने लगा था मैं।

This does not claim a divine miracle or quote a teaching. The practical change is service and honesty while the material problem remains. Review the diction and narrative logic again in the requested dialect before generation; written lines alone do not prove natural delivery.

A short prayer-shaped hook candidate:

> हनुमान, बस इतना बल देना—
>
> थके हुए का हाथ थाम लूँ।
>
> अपने डर से भागूँ कब तक?
>
> आज किसी के काम आऊँ।

Test the melody and breath length rather than presuming this four-line draft is already a hit. Compare alternative hook phrasing in a real take before selecting it; written rhyme alone cannot establish melodic fit.

## Direct the performance instead of overloading adjectives

Begin with a conversational lead that seems reluctant to admit the missed call. Let the question at the hook open up; deliver the act of service with quiet certainty rather than an unrelated shout. Pause after the admission on the temple steps so it can register. A restrained melodic refrain can contrast with denser spoken verses.

Mark breaths and accents in the performance notes, not inside the canonical sung text. Draft two cadences for a difficult line: a longer pickup into the main stress, and a shorter wording with a deliberate rest. Speak them over the pulse if possible; if no actual audition occurred, keep the decision provisional. Do not claim an exact syllable grid or accents from text alone.

At 90 BPM, 44 bars of 4/4 is about 117.33 seconds before pickups/holds. One planning option is 4 intro + 8 verse + 4 hook + 8 verse + 4 hook + 4 bridge + 8 final hook + 4 outro. This is a duration budget, not a lyric timestamp map or an instruction to repeat lines solely to fill bars. Reallocate after hearing the song.
