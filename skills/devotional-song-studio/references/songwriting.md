# Reference-driven devotional writing

## Reference analysis

For each reference record the source, access method, useful traits, and uncertainties. Listen when possible; a title or artist name is insufficient evidence. Analyze:

- Story perspective, opening question, conflict, turning point, and emotional change.
- Cadence, breath lengths, rhyme density, tempo feel, refrain length, and rap/melody contrast.
- Instrument palette, space around vocals, percussion, drops, and call-and-response.
- Why a moment works and how to serve that function with an original lyric, scene, rhythm, or arrangement.

Do not turn analysis into a near-copy. Use source audio as a Gemini upload when the user's requested workflow includes that use; otherwise analyze locally and send the original creative brief. Ancient text and a modern translation, recording, or arrangement have distinct provenance. Track the actual source.

In `reference-analysis.md`, map each useful reference trait to an original execution: for example, an intimate opening becomes a new concrete scene; a crowd response becomes a new refrain; an energy lift becomes a different arrangement change. Identify which source traits conflict and choose deliberately rather than averaging every reference into a generic song.

## Story shapes

Choose for the subject instead of inserting deity names into generic motivational lyrics:

- **Devotee's journey:** concrete everyday struggle → failed coping → encounter or remembered teaching → action inspired by devotion → changed life. Make the final refrain carry new meaning.
- **Scriptural episode:** establish place/stakes → consequential choice → divine encounter → consequence → present-day reflection. Verify the episode/tradition; mark poetic additions.
- **Dialogue or prayer:** question → doubt → insight as poetic interpretation → renewed commitment. Do not label invented divine speech a scriptural quotation.
- **Festival anthem:** communal response with distinct narrative verses; earn the energy through the story.
- **Meditative piece:** recurring image, restrained language, gradual emotional change; rap is optional.

For each concept give a premise, visual motif, sample original refrain, and emotional payoff. Select for specificity, clarity, singability, and fit to references.

For the chosen concept, write a one-sentence causal spine: “Because [event], [person] chooses [action], which leads to [consequence], so [change].” If the verses can swap order without affecting the story, strengthen the causal progression.

Draft three short hook candidates with distinct functions: an intimate prayer, a communal response, or a statement transformed by the ending. Compare their natural pronunciation, immediate meaning, recall, and connection to the central story. Keep the strongest; do not force all three into the song.

## Lyrics and performance

Use the requested language's idiom. For Hindi, favor clear spoken Hindi with intentional Sanskrit vocabulary. Preserve Devanagari; provide Roman transliteration only when useful. For another language adapt idiom and rhyme, rather than translating Hindi word for word; flag pronunciation uncertainty.

Use flexible sections such as cold open, verse, pre-hook, hook, second verse, bridge, final hook, and outro. Each verse advances the story. Keep breathing room and test awkward consonant clusters aloud. Separate pauses, emphasis, pronunciation notes, and call-and-response instructions from sung words.

Duration is an arrangement goal. Estimate section timing, then revise against generated audio. BPM does not prove performed duration. Do not cram a full narrative into a short limit or call disconnected clips a seamless song.

Perform a concrete revision pass: replace stock filler with an image or action, remove lines that exist only to complete a rhyme, shorten breathless phrases, and keep a stable narrator. Divine imagery should express the specific story. Avoid repeated generic claims of darkness/light/power where a place, gesture, object, or choice would convey the emotion. A familiar devotional phrase may remain when it has a deliberate musical or cultural purpose.

## Generation prompt

Save the exact prompt before submitting. Adapt this contract:

```text
Create an ORIGINAL devotional [rap/song] in [language + script].
Subject/tradition: [...]. Story perspective and arc: [...].
Mood progression: [...]. Tempo feel/BPM target: [...].
Instrumentation: [...]. Vocal traits: [...].
Target length: [...] within selected UI capability.
Arrangement: [sections, energy changes, refrain returns, resolved ending].
Use the lyrics below as closely as the tool supports. Labels and pronunciation
notes are instructions, not words to sing. Keep sacred names clear,
vocals intelligible, and the ending complete. Do not imitate a named performer
or reuse an existing hook/melody.

LYRICS:
[sectioned original lyrics]

PRONUNCIATION / PERFORMANCE:
[short targeted notes]
```

Shorten to fit a UI limit while retaining the full local brief. Compare any rewritten words to the actual result; decide whether to revise or accept the sung text. Never caption intended lyrics over different sung words.

## Take selection

Compare narrative completion, lyric fidelity, pronunciation, devotional fit, refrain recall, and audio quality. Missing critical lines or badly pronounced central names outweigh a stronger beat. Make a targeted improvement per retry within the budget. Do not claim native-speaker review or listening that did not occur.
